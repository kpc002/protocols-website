**RNA extraction, cDNA synthesis, and RT-qPCR**

**Qiagen RNAeasy mini kit- Cell lysis and spin column**

1.  If purifying RNA from cell lines rich in RNases, or tissue, add either 10 µL β-mercaptoethanol (β-ME), or 20 µL 2 M dithiothreitol (DTT), to 1 mL Buffer RLT. Buffer RLT with β-ME or DTT can be stored at room temperature for up to 1 month.

2.  Preassemble RNeasy® Mini spin columns with Waste Tubes (provided).

3.  Make sure the ethanol has been added to buffer prior to starting

4.  Harvest a maximum of 1e7 cells, as a cell pellet or by direct lysis in the vessel.

5.  Add 350 ul of Buffer RLT supplemented with BME and pipette vigorously to disrupt the cell pellet. Can pause and freeze at -80 at this step. Keep cold.

6.  If you freeze cells in RLT buffer, thaw at 37C quickly.

7.  Apply 350 ul of cells/RLT buffer to a Qiashredder and spin at max speed for 2 min

8.  Add 1 volume (350 ul if used 350 ul RLT lysis buffer) of 70% ethanol to the 350 ul of RLT that passed through the Qiashredder.

9.  Mix well by pipetting, do not centrifuge.

10. Transfer up to 700 µL of the sample, including any precipitate, to a RNeasy Mini spin column placed in a 2 mL waste tube (supplied). Close the lid, and centrifuge for 15 s at ≥8000 x g. Discard the flow-through and reuse the waste tube.

**Dnase digestion**

Follow steps 1–4 of “Appendix D: Optional On Column DNase Digestion with the RNase Free DNase” in the QIAwave RNA Mini Handbook if using cultured cells to remove genomic DNA. These steps are:

1.  Prepare DNase I stock solution before using kit for first time by dissolving lyophilized DNase I in 550 ul of RNase-free water. Do not open vial, inject water into vial using needle and syringe. Mix gently by inverting. Do not vortex. Let sit at RT for 10 min to dissolve.

2.  For long-term storage, aliquot and store at -20. Do not refreeze aliquots.

3.  After the sample has been spun through the RNeasy mini spin column, add 350 ul of buffer RW1 to the spin column. Close lid gently and centrifuge for 15 s at 8000g to wash column. Discard flowthrough

4.  Add 10 ul of DNase I stock solution made above to 70 ul of buffer RDD for each sample. We made 800 ul total- 100 ul of DNase I and 700 ul of buffer RDD for 8 samples. Mix stock by inverting tube and centrifuge briefly to collect residual liquid from sides of tube. Do not vortex\!

5.  Add 80 ul of DNase I mix directly to RNeasy spin column membrane and incubate at RT for 15 min. Make sure to add DNase incubation mix directly to column membrane as digestion will be incomplete if it sticks to the column membrane.

**Column wash and RNA elution**

6.  Add 350 ul of buffer RW1 to RNeasy spin column. Close lid gently, centrifuge for 15 sec at 8000xg. Discard flow through.

7.  Add 500 ul of buffer RPE to spin column. Close lid gently, centrifuge for 15 sec at 8000xg. Discard flow through.

8.  Add 500 ul of buffer RPE to spin column. Close lid gently, centrifuge for 15 sec at 8000xg. Discard flow through.

9.  Place spin column in a new collection tube and spin at full speed for 1 min to dry membrane.

10. Place spin column in a new 1.5 ml collection tube with lid and add 30 ul of rNase free water directly to spin column membrane. Close lid, centrifuge for 1 min at 8000xg to elute RNA. If low amount of RNA, can incubate at RT for 3 min prior to 1 min spin.

11. For second elution step, add 30 ul of eluted RNA in step 10 back onto the column to increase RNA yield. Remember to incubate at RT for 3 min again before spin.

**cDNA synthesis –** Applied Bio High Capacity cDNA reverse transcriptase

1.  Allow kit components to thaw on ice

2.  Calculate the volume needed to prepare the fllowing reactions on ice

3.  Prepare 2x reverse transcription master mix and place on ice when done. Make sure to make a no RT control where you don’t add reverse transcriptase and just add water to see how much junk there is.

<!-- end list -->

  - 10x RT buffer: 2 ul per 10 ul reaction

  - 25x dNTP mix (100 mM): 0.8 ul per 10 ul reaction

  - 10x RT random primers: 2 ul per 10 ul reaction

  - Multiscribe reverse transcriptase: 1 ul per 10 ul reaction

  - Nuclease-free H20: 4.2 ul per 10 ul reaction

<!-- end list -->

4.  Mix 2x mix gently

5.  Pipette 10 ul of 2x RT master mix into each well/PCR tube

6.  Pipette 10 ul of RNA sample into each well/tube. Pipette up and down to mix

7.  Seal plate/tubes

8.  Briefly centrifuge tubes to spin down contents and eliminate air bubbles

9.  Place plate/tubes on ice until ready to load in thermal cycler.
    
      - Thermal cycler settings: Step 1: 25C for 10 min. Step 2: 37C for 120 min. Step 3: 85C for 5 min. Step 4: 4C for hold

10. Set reaction volume to 20 ul

11. Load and start run.

**Flow to check for Thy1 knockout**

1.  > Move cells from culture plate to v-bottom 96 well plate for flow

2.  > Wash 1x with FACS

3.  > Resuspend in 50 ul of master mix for 20 min at 4C
    
    1.  > L/D APC-Cy7 (1:1000)
    
    2.  > CD8b FITC (1:100)
    
    3.  > Thy1.2 BV510 (1:100)

4.  > Wash 2x with FACS

5.  > Flow

**qPCR**

From this point onwards, work on ice.

**Primer Dilution Prep**

1.  Determine how much primer is needed in qPCR reaction (1.25 ul FWD, 1.25 ul RVS primer per 1x master mix)

2.  Dilute primers 1:5 in nuclease-free water

3.  Vortex briefly and spin down each tube. These are your working primer solutions.

**qPCR Reaction Setup**

**Prepare Reaction Mix:**

1.  Calculate the number of reactions needed per gene target (samples + controls + extras for pipetting error).

2.  Prepare a master mix for each gene target (Target Gene and Housekeeping Gene) separately to ensure consistency. For *N* reactions, mix the following in a fresh tube:
    
      - SYBR Green Master Mix (2x): 12.5 μl \* N
    
      - Respective Diluted Primer Mix: 1.25 μl \* N
    
      - Nuclease-free water: (27 – MM which is 15 ul) = 12 ul ddH20

3.  Vortex the master mix gently and spin down.

4.  Aliquot 27 μl of the appropriate master mix into each well of your qPCR plate/tube.

5.  Dilute cDNA samples 1:5 with nuclease-free water (3 ul of cDNA with 12 ul H2O)

6.  Add 3 μl of cDNA sample to the corresponding wells. For NTC wells, add 3 μl of nuclease-free water instead of cDNA. Total reaction volume per well: 30 μl. For the no RT wells, add samples prepared in cDNA synthesis step and master mix. Total reaction volume per well: 30 ul.

7.  Seal the qPCR plate/tubes securely. Briefly centrifuge the plate/tubes to collect the contents at the bottom of the wells and remove air bubbles.

**qPCR Instrument Run**

6.  Place the plate/tubes into the qPCR machine.

7.  Use Angelica’s standard SYBR Green protocol suitable for your master mix and instrument. A typical protocol is:
    
      - **Initial Denaturation:** 95°C for 2-10 minutes (check master mix recommendations)
    
      - **Cycling (e.g., 40 cycles):**
        
          - Denaturation: 95°C for 10-30 seconds
        
          - Annealing/Extension: 60-65°C for 30-60 seconds (ensure fluorescence data acquisition at this step)
    
      - **Melt Curve Analysis:** Standard melt curve program (e.g., 95°C for 15s, 60°C for 60s, then ramp slowly to 95°C with continuous fluorescence acquisition). This helps check for primer dimers or non-specific amplification.
    
      - **Optimization**: Cycling conditions, especially the annealing/extension temperature and time, may need optimization based on the specific primers, target sequence, and master mix used. Primer efficiency should ideally be validated.

**Data Analysis**

This describes the common [<span class="underline">Livak method</span>](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11846609/) (ΔΔCt).

1.  **Obtain Ct Values:** Export the cycle threshold (Ct) values for all samples and controls for both the Target Gene and Housekeeping Gene from the qPCR software.
    
      - **Validate Controls:** Ensure NTCs show no or very late amplification (e.g., Ct \> 35). Check No RT controls for minimal signal compared to cDNA samples.
    
      - **Check Melt Curves:** Verify single, specific peaks at the expected melting temperature for each gene.

2.  **Calculate ΔCt (Delta Ct) for each sample:** Normalize the Ct value of the Target Gene to the Ct value of the Housekeeping Gene for each sample. ΔCt = Ct<sub>(Target Gene)</sub> - Ct<sub>(Housekeeping Gene)</sub>

3.  **Select a Control/Reference Sample:** Choose one sample (or the average of a control group) as the reference calibrator (e.g., untreated sample, time point zero).

4.  **Calculate ΔΔCt (Delta-Delta Ct):** Calculate the difference between the ΔCt of each sample and the ΔCt of the reference/calibrator sample. ΔΔCt = ΔCt<sub>(Sample)</sub> - ΔCt<sub>(Reference Sample)</sub>

**Step 1: Open the Completed Run File**

1.  Open the **CFX Maestro** or **CFX Manager** software on the computer connected to your system.

2.  Click on the **View Post-Run Data** tab or go to File \> Open \> Data File.

3.  Select your completed .pcrd run file and click **Analyze Data**.

**Step 2: View and Format the Data Sheets**

1.  By default, the software opens to the **Quantification** tab (showing your standard thermal cycling curves).

2.  Click on the **Melt Curve** tab next to it.

3.  Look at the lower half of the screen to find the data spreadsheets associated with that tab. You will see tabs like Melt Peaks, RFU Spreadsheet, and -d(RFU)/dT Spreadsheet. 

**Step 3: Export All Data Sheets to Excel**

The absolute fastest way to get all your thermal cycle and melt curve matrices at once is to export the entire workbook:

1.  In the main top menu bar of the software, click on **Export**.

2.  From the dropdown menu, select **Export All Data Sheets to Excel**.

3.  Choose a destination folder (such as a USB drive or local directory), name your file, and click **Save**.
