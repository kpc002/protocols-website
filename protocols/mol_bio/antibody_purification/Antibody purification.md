Purification of Antibodies:

Equipment:

HiTrap Protein G column –

Peristaltic Pump or syringe

Collection tubes (FACS tubes work really well) with 100 ul of Tris pH 9.0

Protocol:

Important things to remember:

Peristaltic Pump:

  - Run Sodium Phosphate buffer pH 7.0 into the tubing to equilibrate it until it begins dripping out the side with the metal lock. Stop the flow.

  - Place column onto a metal stand

  - Attach the metal lock to the column using the red adaptor then continue the flow. This is known as adding it ‘drop-to-drop’, which prevents air from entering the column.

  - Run 5x the volume of the column (25 ml) of Sodium Phosphate buffer pH 7.0 over the column and save the flow through.

  - Begin running your antibody supernatant over the column…make sure to use a new collection tube once the supernatant reaches the column.

  - Once all of your supernatant has been collected, run another 5 volumes of Sodium Phosphate buffer pH 7.0.

  - Elute the antibody using 0.1M Glycine buffer, pH 2.8. 20 fractions of approximately 1 ml each is good. For this small a volume, 30 ml syringes work well to add the glycine. Just make sure to put the syringe on ‘drop-to-drop’ to avoid air entering the column.

  - Once eluted, attach the column onto the metal stand once again, and run 5 bed volumes of 20% EtOH through it. The column can be stored at 4°C until later use. The tubing can stay attached until later use.

  - Set up a 96-well plate with the following for each fraction (don’t forget pos and neg controls too\!):
    
      - 20 ul sample
    
      - 20 ul biorad protein assay dye (Cat \# 500-0006)
    
      - 60 ul H20

<!-- end list -->

  - Collect the fractions that turn the darkest blue – these contain the eluted antibody\!

  - Use spin collection tubes to concentrate the antibody (see tube directions for how)

  - Use a protein assay kit (BioRad) to analyze the concentration of your antibody.
