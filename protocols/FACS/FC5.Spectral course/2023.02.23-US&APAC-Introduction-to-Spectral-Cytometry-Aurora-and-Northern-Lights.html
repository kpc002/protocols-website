Quantification of the Impact of Spread on Resolution

Cross Stain Index (CSI)

  • Cross Stain Index (CSI) is a calculation of the stain index of a fluorochrome in the presence or absence of the other fluorochromes in the panel.
  • Stain Index Reduction (SIR) is the percent reduction in resolution of a fluorochrome due to the spread imparted by another fluorochrome in the panel.
  • SIR values are influenced by antigen density and fluorochrome brightness.

Guidelines for Assigning Fluorochromes in a Panel

Use the same panel design rules as conventional cytometry

Tertiary antigens

    1. Assess reagent availability (often not too many options available)
    1. Assign brightest fluorochrome available (use fluorochrome brightness ranking)

Secondary antigens 2

    1. Based on co-expression of antigens expressed at intermediate levels
    1. If no co-expression, use any bright dye still available
    1. If co-expression:
  • Use a bright dye that does not spread into selected fluorochrome for tertiary antigens
  • If only available dyes have spread, use a dim dye to minimize spread impact

Primary antigens

    1. Often available in many colors
    1. Assign to dyes that are dim and that have minimal spread in other dyes (examples: FITC, Pacific Blue, BV510, Alexa Fluor 532, APC-H7)

Titration: The First Step in Panel Optimization

  •  Titration needs to be done before using the reagents (antibodies and functional dyes) in a multicolor experiment.
  •  It helps find the concentration of antibody that best resolves a positive signal from the background.
  •  It is necessary for each experimental system based on cell type/tissue, cell preparation conditions, staining protocol, and reagent lot/clones used.
  •  The use of an incorrect antibody titer or a nonsaturating dilution can lead to poor resolution, non-specific binding, high spread, waste of reagents, and bad data quality.

How to Adjust Gains Correctly

Recommended Steps

  •  Identify the peak channel for the fluorochrome that is off scale
  •  Use the ‘All Channels %’ box to decrease the gain value proportionally in all detectors for that laser module
  •  Apply same ‘All Channels %’ value for all modules to maintain the spectrum of every dyes
