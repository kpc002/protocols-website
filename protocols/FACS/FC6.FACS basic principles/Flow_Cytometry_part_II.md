## Slide 10 — Critical parameters affecting

![Slide 10: Critical parameters affecting](Flow_Cytometry_part_II_files/slide-10.jpg)

<details>
<summary>Extracted slide text</summary>

<pre>Critical parameters affecting
                    titers
• TIME: the longer the incubation the lower the titer needed to reach
  saturation
• TEMPERATURE: some Abs stain better at certain temperatures, e.g.
  chemokine receptors give a much higher fluorescence when stained at
  37°C compared to RT or 4°C
• BUFFER: intracellular staining (Fix/Perm buffers) can alter the
  optimized titer  titrate antibodies with the buffer used in the staining


Cell number DOES NOT affect the Ab titer. The Ab is always in vast
excess to the number of molecules, unless cell number changes
dramatically.


General rule: the optimized titer can be used to stain up to 10-15</pre>

</details>

<details>
<summary>Extracted slide text</summary>

<pre>Aspects to consider
•   Compensation can be performed manually (at the
    machine) or automatically (via software)

•   Manual compensation: tedious, need to check carefully
    that positive and negative signals have to match

•   Automated compensation: no errors, if using the right
    controls

•   Manual compensation is impractical in a multicolor
    experiment (roughly 2n pairwise combinations)

•   Changing compensation in a pairwise combination
    affects distribution in other channels.</pre>

## Slide 23 — The 3 golden rules of

![Slide 23: The 3 golden rules of](Flow_Cytometry_part_II_files/slide-23.jpg)

<details>
<summary>Extracted slide text</summary>

<pre>The 3 golden rules of
             compensation
1. Use a single-stained control that is AT LEAST
   as bright as the test staining

2. Use the same reagent that is being used in
   the staining (valid for tandems such as PE-
   Cy7, APC-Cy7, etc. – for non tandems any
   reagent is OK)

3. Have a paired-negative control with the
   SAME autofluorescence as the positive (cells
   with cells, beads with beads, etc.)

Everything else is not necessary!</pre>

</details>

## Slide 32 — “Errors” in compensation

![Slide 32: “Errors” in compensation](Flow_Cytometry_part_II_files/slide-32.jpg)

<details>
<summary>Extracted slide text</summary>

<pre>“Errors” in compensation

• Properly compensated data may not appear
  rectilinear because of measurement errors (are
  more prominent in case of limited numbers of
  photons, Cy7PE or Cy7APC dyes, with low
  energy)

• Measurement errors are directly proportional to
  the intensity (LOG relationship)

• This effect on compensated data is unavoidable,
  and cannot be corrected with compensation
  (LINEAR correction)

• Compensation does not introduce the error, it only
  REVEALS it because it brings the distribution to the</pre>

</details>
