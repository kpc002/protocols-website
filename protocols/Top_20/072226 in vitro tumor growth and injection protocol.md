**In vitro tumor growth and injection protocol 07/22/26**

**Nicole Scharping**

## A. Preparing frozen tumor cells for inoculation

1. Tumor cells are stored in the liquid nitrogen storage tank or −150°C chest freezer. Use the storage map to locate the cells and remove the vial from the map once it has been removed from storage.
2. Carefully and quickly remove the frozen vial(s) from the storage rack, then return the rack to the liquid nitrogen tank.
3. Rapidly thaw tubes in a 37°C water bath or on the skirt of the hood, then move them to the hood.
4. Open the vial(s), remove the thawed medium with a 1 mL pipette, and transfer to a culture dish (15 mm). Add the appropriate cell medium (R10, D10, or other) in a volume that covers the plate (20 mL for a 15 mm plate).
5. Place the plate in a culture incubator and grow cells until the plate is approximately two-thirds to three-quarters confluent. The appropriate confluence may vary by tumor-cell type and may require optimization.
6. When cells have reached the desired confluence:

   - Remove the plate from the incubator and carefully aspirate the medium.
   - Wash once with cold sterile PBS and aspirate.
   - Add 5 mL trypsin and incubate in the cell-culture incubator for approximately 5 minutes at 37°C.
   - Add 5 mL medium. Gently wash the plate with a pipette while tilting it to release cells from the plastic surface.
   - Collect the cell suspension in a 15 mL conical tube and remove a 10 µL aliquot to count cells. Spin the tube while counting.

## B. Tumor-cell injections

1. Resuspend cells at the concentration appropriate to the experiment, based on a 100 µL injection volume. The source gives 250,000 cells per injection for B16-GP33 and 1 × 10<sup>6</sup> cells per injection for MC38-GP33 as examples. Resuspend cells in PBS or medium for injection.
2. Transfer cells to Eppendorf tubes or 50 mL conical tubes that accommodate a syringe, and place on ice in disposable tip boxes for transport to the animal facility.
3. Use 28-gauge insulin syringes. The source suggests approximately one syringe per cage because needles lose sharpness with repeated injections.
4. In the animal-facility procedure room, prepare the anesthesia chamber:

   - Place the clear plastic chamber in the hood.
   - Check oxygen-tank and isoflurane levels; replace or refill as appropriate.
   - Open the oxygen tank and set isoflurane to 3 or 3.5. Confirm that oxygen is flowing and that the vaporizer reads 1.5–2.0 psi.
   - Place clean paper towels in the chamber, place mice inside, and wait for slow, deep breathing and no movement.
   - Remove mice, shave the back area with clippers, and return them to the chamber. Ear punches may be performed at this stage for identification.
5. Ensure that tumor cells are resuspended by inverting the tube or vortexing. Draw 100 µL cell suspension for each mouse into a syringe.
6. For subdermal injection, position the back skin by rolling it over a finger or pulling it upward, then insert the needle with the bevel facing up.
7. Inject 100 µL subdermally, avoiding puncture through the skin that could allow tumor-cell medium to leak and cause unequal doses.
8. A large bubble should form on the mouse's back. After injection, weigh the mouse and record its weight, then return it to its home cage. Keep completed cages outside the hood until all mice have recovered before returning cages to the animal room.
9. After inoculations are complete, return cages to the animal room and put away the isoflurane vaporizer apparatus. If the holding chamber is soiled with urine or feces, wash it; the source instructs not to use ethanol or other cleaners that damage the plastic.

## C. Monitor mouse weight and tumor growth

1. Monitor tumor growth and begin animal treatments when tumors reach the size or date appropriate to the experiment.
2. Record observations and mouse weight at each monitoring step. The source states that monitoring and paper records are required by UCSD and must be kept in the mouse room.
3. Use the following tumor-stage observation schedule from the source:

   - **Stage 1:** No visible, palpable, clinical, or behavioral signs of tumor burden — monitor weekly.
   - **Stage 2:** Visible or palpable tumors, or changes in appearance or behavior — monitor twice weekly.
   - **Stage 3:** Any signs of morbidity or mortality, or subcutaneous tumor diameter greater than 1.5 cm — monitor daily. The source states that animals must be euthanized when a tumor reaches 2 cm in any direction.
