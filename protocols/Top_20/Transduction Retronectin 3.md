This page combines two Retronectin-based transduction workflows. The Retronectin loading volumes, spin duration, virus-production instructions, and post-transduction handling differ. Select one complete source-specific option for each difference and confirm current lab-approved conditions before use.

## Source files

- **Alex Monell** — [Transduction - High Quality](../Transfection%20and%20Transduction/Retronectin/Transduction%20-%20High%20Quality.md)
- **Nicole Sharping** — [NES 042924 CD8 T cell transduction and double transduction](../Transfection%20and%20Transduction/Plat_E/NES%20042924%20CD8%20T%20cell%20transduction%20and%20double%20transduction.md)

## 1. Prepare virus and activated CD8 T cells

### Virus-production and detailed activation route

**Derived from: Nicole Sharping.**

1. Thaw and expand Plat-E cells beginning five days before transduction, then plate 2 × 10<sup>6</sup> cells per 10 cm plate two days before transduction.
2. Transfect Plat-E cells with 2 µg pCL-Eco, 6 µg total plasmid of interest, and 30 µL TransIT-LT1 in 700 µL serum-free DMEM. For double transduction, divide the 6 µg total plasmid across the plasmids of interest.
3. Coat flat-bottom 6-well plates with anti-CD3 (5 µg/mL for P14 or 10 µg/mL for wild-type CD8 cells), then activate isolated CD8 T cells with anti-CD28 and 50 U/mL hIL-2 for at least 24 hours.

### Activation-only route

**Derived from: Alex Monell.**

Begin with CD8 T-cell isolation and activation. Alex’s source does not include packaging-cell or virus-production instructions.

## 2. Coat and block Retronectin plates

### Alex Monell coating route

**Derived from: Alex Monell.**

1. Use a non-tissue-culture-treated 6-well plate.
2. Prepare sufficient Retronectin mixture for the number of wells plus one extra well, add 1 mL per well, wrap the plate carefully with parafilm, and incubate at 4°C overnight.
3. On the transduction day, remove Retronectin, add 4 mL warmed TCM dropwise to each well, and block for 30 minutes at 37°C.

### Nicole Sharping coating route

**Derived from: Nicole Sharping.**

1. Prepare Retronectin at 15 µg/mL in sterile PBS and add 1 mL per well of a non-tissue-culture-treated 6-well plate.
2. Seal the plate and incubate at 4°C overnight.
3. Remove Retronectin, add 4 mL R10 dropwise to block nonspecific sites, and incubate for 30 minutes at 37°C while warming the centrifuge to 37°C.

## 3. Load virus and transduce cells

### Alex Monell transduction route

**Derived from: Alex Monell.**

1. Thaw virus in a hot-water bath, then keep it on ice once thawed.
2. Remove blocking TCM, add 750 µL virus per well, and incubate for 30 minutes at 37°C. Replace with a fresh 750 µL virus aliquot and incubate another 30 minutes.
3. Pool cells, pellet at 1600 RPM for 4 minutes at room temperature, and prepare TCM plus IL-2 at the source’s stated 1:1667 dilution.
4. Add 500 µL cell suspension and 1.5 mL virus to each well. Spin at 1600 RPM and 37°C for 40 minutes, then incubate at 37°C for 24 hours.

### Nicole Sharping transduction route

**Derived from: Nicole Sharping.**

1. Collect viral medium from Plat-E cells and pellet floating packaging cells.
2. Remove blocking R10, add 1 mL virus per well for 30 minutes at 37°C, then repeat with a second 1 mL virus aliquot for 30 minutes.
3. Collect activated T cells, pellet them, and resuspend them in remaining viral medium with 50 U/mL IL-2.
4. Add cells to virus-coated wells, wrap the plate in cellophane, and spin at 1600 RPM and 37°C for 45 minutes. Incubate overnight, then replace medium with fresh R10 plus IL-2 the next morning.

## 4. Follow up after transduction

### Alex Monell sorting and transfer route

**Derived from: Alex Monell.**

On Day 3, collect cell populations separately, wash wells with MACS buffer, pellet at 1600 RPM for 4 minutes, resuspend in MACS buffer, filter cells, sort them into FACS tubes with FBS, wash sorted cells, resuspend in PBS, and count before preparing for transfer.

### Nicole Sharping culture route

**Derived from: Nicole Sharping.**

Maintain cells at 1 × 10<sup>6</sup>/mL with R10 plus IL-2. At 5–6 days after activation, reduce IL-2 to 25 U/mL. Nicole’s source describes proliferation through days 7–10 and viability through days 10–14 after activation.
