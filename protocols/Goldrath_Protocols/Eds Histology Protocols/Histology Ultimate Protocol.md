**Epic Histology Protocol – for Spleen and Lymph Nodes**

**<span class="underline">Tissue Processing</span>**

<span class="underline"></span>

**Materials**

  - 4% Formaldehyde/Paraformaldehyde(PFA) – see PFA protocol
    
      - Make fresh from high concentration stock each time
    
      - Use 15 conicals – enough to cover spleen/LN

  - 5% Sucrose/ 4% formaldehyde (4mL sucrose, 3mL 37% formaldehyde, 20mL PBS)

  - 10% Sucrose

  - 30% Sucrose

  - OCT, Cryo-molds cassette, and dry ice

  - Fisherbrand Superfrost-Plus microscope slides

  - 1x PBS

**Method**

<span class="underline">Fixation and Dehydration</span>

1.  Sacrifice mouse and harvest spleen/ lymph nodes.

2.  Drop organs into 4% formaldehyde/5% sucrose conicals **<span class="underline">IMMEDIATELY</span>** upon removal and place on ice.
    
    1.  Attach tubes to BellyDancer or something with rotation, or periodically rotate/tap to make sure fixative penetrates all directions.
    
    2.  Leave organs in fixative (4% formaldehyde) at least a **combined** 6 hours at 4°C
    
    3.  Incubate in 5% Sucrose for at least 3 hours.

<!-- end list -->

  - Aspirate out the fixative and pour in 9 mL of 10% Sucrose solution (at 4% formaldehyde is tissues have not been incubated for at least 6 hrs yet)
    
    1.  Incubate for 2-3 hrs at 4°C.

<!-- end list -->

3.  Replace 10% Sucrose with 30% Sucrose and incubate at 4 degree C° overnight.

<span class="underline"></span>

<span class="underline">Embedding</span>

4.  Put processed organs into respective small Petri dishes with fresh OCT and use to:
    
    1.  Wash residual sucrose off by rolling around in OCT.

5.  Cover bottom of **Cry-mold cassette** with a layer of OCT
    
    1.  Spleen: Rest on dry ice, and as OCT starts to freeze (white streaks form), immediately place spleen into cassette so OCT will start gripping spleen
        
        1.  Quickly cover the spleen with OCT by filling cassette with copious amounts of OCT
        
        2.  Leave on dry ice until completely frozen
    
    2.  Lymph Nodes: First place lymph nodes into bottom of cassette with liquid OCT, then place on dry ice until bottom begins to freeze
        
        1.  When frozen OCT begins to grip lymph nodes, quickly cover lymph nodes in cassette with copious amounts of OCT
        
        2.  Leave on dry ice until completely frozen

6.  Store all blocks in labeled boxes in -80°C.

**<span class="underline">Fixation and Slide Preparation for Staining</span>**

<span class="underline"></span>

**Materials**

  - Fixing reagent of choice: **-20°C** **Acetone**, **4%** **Formaldehyde** (Stock 37% Formaldehyde w/ 5-10% methanol as preservative), **4%** **Paraformaldehyde** (**PFA**, 100% pure formaldehyde)
    
      - See protocol for making 20% PFA (4% PFA easily degrades)

  - Coplin jars for acetone fixations

  - 1X PBS (ridiculous amounts) – use 10X PBS for dilutions rather than 1X stocks

  - Coplin jars or slide holder chambers (for many samples) for washes

  - Glass slide holder (compatible with slide holder chamber)

  - Pap pen (creates liquid repelling barrier around sample)

  - Large Kimwipes

  - **Thawed** sample slides (no ice crystal damage)

<span class="underline"></span>

**Method**

<span class="underline"></span>

<span class="underline">Fixation (pick one of following fixatives): After samples are **thawed**</span>

1.  **-20°C Acetone** – <span class="underline">Harshest</span> but cleanest fixation method, will destroy GFP and other very sensitive epitopes (protein pulldown/precipitation reaction)/ Note: acetone will smear off all permanent markers
    
    1.  Pour in enough acetone to **cold** coplin jar to cover **sections on** slides, AT **MOST** UP TILL THE FROSTED EDGE WITH WRITING (acetone **will** smear)
    
    2.  Carefully transport to -20°C and fix for **5 minutes**
    
    3.  Carefully pull out slides individually and place on paper towels, allowing acetone to quickly evaporate
    
    4.  After evaporation, wash 3x with 1X PBS in coplin jars, 10-15 minutes each

2.  **4% Formaldehyde/ PFA** – standard fixation, for more sensitive epitopes
    
    1.  Dilute high concentration formaldehyde/PFA stock to 4% in PBS
        
        1.  \~50uL/section on each slide (Exp: 3 sections/slide = \~150uL)
    
    2.  Pipet \~50uL onto each sample at RT, careful not to damage section
    
    3.  Fix for **10 minutes**
    
    4.  Wash 3x with 1X PBS in coplin jars, 5-10 minutes each time

<span class="underline">Pap Penning and Preparation for Blocking/Staining</span>

3.  Lay out slides on paper towels and dry around the glass and **individual sections** using large Kimwipes (paper towels will form lint). DO NOT TOUCH SECTIONS WITH TOWELS\!

4.  Circle each individual section a few times with pap pen, allow to dry

5.  Dip sections into PBS again to retain moisture

**<span class="underline">Blocking</span>**

**Materials**

  - Blocking Buffer – the Epic (Gelatin) Block (for 50mL)
    
      - 1.5mL normal goat serum (3%)
    
      - 1.5mL normal donkey serum (3%)
    
      - 1.5-2.0 g BSA
    
      - 1mL fishscale gelatin (advised to add after some PBS)
    
      - 25ul Tween20 (0.05%)
    
      - 50ul Triton-X-100 (0.1%)
    
      - 250ul 10% Azide (0.05%)
    
      - Fill to the 50ml mark w/ 1x PBS
    
      - Optional: make \~5 tubes and aliquot into 15mL conicals and freeze to store

  - Fc Block

  - Vector Labs Avidin/Biotin blocking kit (if using biotinylated antibodies)

  - Hydration chamber (basically some sort of plastic sealable container with **wet** paper towels on the bottom – incubate slides so liquid does not evaporate)

  - Coplin jars or slide chambers (see fixation materials)

  - 1x PBS

**Method**

1.  Make master mix: depending on section size, add \~50uL Gelatin Block and \~5uL Fc block per sample
    
    1.  Pipet \~50uL onto pap pen circled section, making sure to cover section

2.  Place slides into hydration chamber and incubate for 30 to 60 minutes

3.  Rinse off each slide by dipping a few times into PBS (in Coplin jars)
    
    1.  Aspirate off extra liquid **carefully** using edges of Kimwipe. DO NOT TOUCH SECTION\!

4.  Only if using biotinylated antibodies – Avidin/Biotin block, perform **in this order**:
    
    1.  Apply 1 drop of avidin block onto each section – incubate \~15 minutes
        
        1.  Rinse off each slide by dipping a few times in PBS
    
    2.  Apply 1 drop of biotin block onto each section – incubate \~15 minutes
        
        1.  Rinse off each slide by dipping a few times in PBS

5.  Slides are ready to stain

**<span class="underline">Staining</span>**

**Materials**

  - Epic (Gelatin) Blocking Buffer (see Blocking)

  - Antibodies (see list at end for antibodies and concentrations tested using this protocol)

  - Coplin jars or slide chambers (see fixation materials)

  - 1x PBS

  - Hydration chamber (see Blocking)

  - 37° Incubator

**Method**

1.  Similar to Blocking, aspirate off extra liquid using kimwipes without letting slides completely dry out

2.  Make master mix of respective Primary Ab (including biotinylated Abs) stains in the blocking buffer
    
    1.  Pipet Ab mixture onto sections \~50ul

3.  Place slides into hydration chamber and seal lid

4.  Incubate slides in hydration chamber in 37° incubator for 1 hour (after 1 hour, antibodies/proteins will start to denature)

5.  After 1 hour, **<span class="underline">EITHER</span>**:
    
    1.  Put slides into PBS in coplin jars/slide chamber and wash 3x at 5-10 minutes each
    
    2.  **<span class="underline">OR</span>**, if working with a difficult Ab, move slides in hydration champer with Ab mixture still on into cold room and incubate for another 2-4 hrs. Afterwards, wash as in 5a.

6.  If no secondary Ab is needed, move onto “Mounting”

7.  Pipet on Secondary Ab mixture prepared same way as the Primary
    
    1.  Follow same steps as Primary Ab
    
    2.  Incubate for only \~30 minutes at 37° (or else high background)

8.  Wash slides 3x in 1x PBS, 5-10 minutes each time

**<span class="underline">Mounting and Sealing</span>**

<span class="underline"></span>

**Materials**

  - Paper Towels

  - Kimwipes

  - Mounting Media – Invitrogen Pro-Long Gold Antifade Reagent (I prefer w/o DAPI)
    
      - Rest upside down to get rid of bubbles

  - Nail Polish (Sally Hansen brand, or anything that isn’t too thick)

  - Fisherbrand glass coverslips

**Method**

1.  Using Kimwipes, wipe slides of all liquid and smear off some pap pen
    
    1.  Be very careful not to touch the section using Kimwipes
    
    2.  Leave slides in dark area like drawer to dry for a few minutes (not too long)

2.  Apply very small drop of Mounting Media onto each section
    
    1.  **NO BUBBLES** – If bubbles form, aspirate off using a pipet

3.  Carefully place on coverslips
    
    1.  Technique: place on one edge first and slowly lower onto the other

4.  Carefully apply nail polish to edges of coverslip to seal onto the slide
    
    1.  Technique: do not move the coverslip; if nail polish is too thick, dilute it down with some acetone

5.  Store slides in 4° until ready to view

**<span class="underline">Random tips</span>**

<span class="underline"></span>

  - 37° incubation might not work for everything
    
      - For more sensitive staining such as KLRG-1 – stain for 1hr in 37° and then transfer to 4° for another 2-3hrs – overnight. Makes a big difference

  - Pay attention to appropriate fixation

  - This protocol works very well on staining for CD11c, CD45.1, CD4, and b220 thus far

  - CD8 staining can be a little weak

  - Thy1.1 is the mother of trouble – stringent tissue processing and careful attention to staining concentrations is key

  - Cutting using revised tissue processing protocol makes placing section onto slide very difficult sometimes
    
      - Use gradual pulling technique (similar to riding the block) by picking off one side of section first and pulling meticulously as it quickly sticks to slide
    
      - Warning: can be incredibly frustrating; use caution when cutting very important experiments
        
          - Lowering concentration of fixative can if these problems ensue, but higher risk of epitope degradation, especially if working with Thy1.1

  - Section a certain way… the longitudinal, not lateral, cuts for the spleen

  - Need a butt load of PBS – suggest using 10x

  - Be a bit more careful w/ working in the light/sunlight after slides are stained

  - **Everything** is variable, whatever works works, and what works for some, will not for others

### Blocking Solutions

   

Background or unspecific staining often occurs for IHC antibody staining. Here we list commonly used blocking solutions during antibody staining procedure to reduce background or unspecific staining.

**Peroxidase Blocking Soluiton (3% H2O2 in PBS):**

       30% H2O2 ------------------------- 10 ml

       1X PBS ----------------------------- 90 ml

       Mix well and store at 4 ºC for up to 3 months.

       Block sections for 10 minutes before blocking step or after primary antibody incubation. Note: this solution is recommended for paraffin sections.

 

**Peroxidase Blocking Soluiton (0.3% H2O2 in Methanol):**

       30% H2O2 ------------------------- 1 ml

       Methanol -------------------------- 99 ml

       Mix well and store at 4 ºC.

       Block sections for 20-30 minutes before serum blocking step prior to primary antibody incubation. Note: this solution is recommended for frozen sections. Blocking after primary antibody incubation may cause tissue damage.

 

**Avidin/Biotin Block:**

       Avidin 0.001% in PBS

       Biotin 0.001% in PBS

       Store these blocking solution at 4 ºC.    

Incubate sections for 10-15 min each and rinse with PBS between steps. Recommended to block before primary antibody incubation.
