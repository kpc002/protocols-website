CALCIUM PHOSPHATE TRANSFECTION OF ΦNX CELLS TO PRODUCE VIRAL SUP.

Day 1: Plate 1 million Phoenix cells per petridish (Falcon 3004; this is a 6cm petridish with a surface area of 21 sq cm) in 4 ml Iscove's containing 10% FCS + 2-ME + antibiotics. Culture cells ON as usual (no shaking/rocking) in a 37C incubator.

Day 2: Ca-P transfection (Gibco BRL 18306-019)

885 ul water

100 ul 10x HBS

vortex

15 ul 1M NaOH

vortex

Pipet 500 ul 1x HBS soln + **10 ul phosphate soln into tube 1**.

Tube 2:

430 ul water

10-20 ug plasmid DNA (prefered conc: 1 ug/ul), mix gently

10 ul Calcium solution, mix gently

50 ul Calcium solution, mix gently

Bubble air in tube 1 and add slowly the mix from tube 2 into tube 1. This will become a milky suspension. Incubate 20 min at RT. Add 10 min before the transfection 4 ul of 30 mM Chloroquine (=15 mg/ml and filter sterile) to the Phoenix cells and mix gently by swirling.

Add the DNA-Ca-P mixture dropwise to the Phoenix cells and incubate them as usual ON at 37C.

Day 3: Replace medium by 4 ml FTOC medium and incubate the Phoenix cells as usual O/N at 37C.

Day 4: Harvest transient virus supernatant. Keep the virus 15 min on ice. Spin 5 minutes 1500 rpm or filter supernatant (to get rid of the few Phoenix cells present in the supernatant; For the FTOC experiments spinning will do the job) and make 0.5 ml or 1 ml aliquots of the viral supernatant. Freeze immediately at -80C. Because freeze steps reduce the transduction efficiency with 50% we thaw an aliquot only once. Phoenix cells are analyzed by FACS for eGFP expression the same day.

What is important, is to do the transfection at the END of day 2 and to replace the medium with FTOC medium at the BEGINNING of day 3. Whatever transfection protocol you will use, the cells won't like it and therefore this period should be as short as possible. Furthermore, replacing the medium at the beginning of day 3 with FTOC medium gives you the full 24h to accumulate virus before harvesting the supernatant at the beginning of day 4.

The 4 ml FTOC medium will appear orange-yellow just before harvesting, so, I would not advice to decrease the volume any further. It may increase your virus titer but I don't think many fetal thymocytes will survive the transduction in such exhausted medium. They already have a hard time surviving the transduction protocol as it is now.

What is also important is that the virus supernatant harvesting procedure is performed as fast as possible. After 15 min on ice immediately spin the supernatant and very quickly freeze at -80C.
