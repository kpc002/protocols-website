**<span class="underline">Fetal Liver Chimeras</span>**

Day 1:

1.  Any timed mating females pregnant on day E14-14.5 can be used.

2.  Sac pregnant female and remove both horns of the uterus from the abdominal cavity.

3.  Cut along the bottom of the horns, avoiding blood from the surrounding vessels (this is maternal blood and can contaminate the fetal samples). Place uterus in a petri dish with RP5 media to rinse.

4.  Remove the embryos from the uterus and amniotic sac. This is most easily done with forceps. Be careful to keep the embryo intact and to avoid contamination with maternal blood.

5.  Place separated embryos in another petri dish with media to rinse.

6.  Store each embryo in a 12 or 24 well plate in a couple of mLs of RP5. Wrap the edges of the plate in parafilm and store at 4C. Be careful to avoid knocking the plate over.

7.  For genotyping take a hand or foot from each embryo. PCRs must be completed as soon as possible.

Day 2:

1.  Remove the fetal liver (using forceps) and place in 500ul of RP5. Be more concerned with getting all of the liver cells, extra tissue taken during this step will be filtered out later.

2.  Pipet up and down vigorously to resuspend the liver cells. Filter sample into a new tube.

3.  Use 5ul to count cells and 5ul to stain for congenic markers (CD45.1 and CD45.2).

4.  When staining fetal liver ALWAYS Fc block for five minutes and stain the cells for a full 15. It’s usually hard to see 45.1 and 45.2 positive cells at this stage.

5.  Select recipient mice based on congenic markers. Mice should be no younger than four weeks, six is better. Males preferable

6.  Irradiate recipient mice with 1000 rad (900 if they are really small).

7.  Process RAGko bone marrow in media (Id2ko mice have a defect in APCs so we use RAGko cells to solve this problem).

8.  Each mouse should receive an absolute minimum of 3x10^6 fetal liver cells. 5x10^6 is optimal. Even up to 10x10^6 is fine. However, normalize the number of fetal liver cells per mouse for each group (EG: make sure recipients getting KO cells get the same number of cells as recipients getting WT cells). Add 1/10<sup>th</sup> the number of RAGko bone marrow cells (EG: if you’re injecting 5x10^6 fetal liver cells add 5x10^5 RAGko bone marrow cells).

9.  Wash the cells with sterile PBS (media can cause rejection. However, since the mice have been irradiated if you have very few cells you can skip this step). Resuspend in correct amount of PBS (each mouse should receive it’s cells in 200ul).

10. Inject cells ASAP into the tail veins of recipient mice (cells don’t do too well in PBS for long periods of time).

11. Recipient mice should receive antibiotics in their drinking water for 2 weeks (changed weekly).
