Listeria production:

Ok, here's the instructions from Phil on making freezes of Listeria

(this is how I make what I sent you):

Hope the cells got to you fine. I asked Ingrid, our lab manager, about

Freezing Listeria, and basically what you want to do is take what the saturated

frozen cultures that you have currently, and inoculate a large BHI culture to grow for

during the day (e.g., 200 ul into 100 ml) until it reaches a log phase OD 600 of \~0.2.

Then just freeze them directly in the BHI in aliquots (we do 1 ml aliquots). You can then take those and thaw them anytime to grow another culture to OD 0.1 to infect mice (I put 200 ul into 5 ml BHI, it takes about 2.5 hrs to get to OD 0.1). Hope this helps.

Here are instructions for growing Listeria:

Thaw a frozen stock (I usually stick in a 37 degree H20 bath). Dilute

1:50 in tryptic soy broth (no more than 1 month old). I usually make

10-20 mls. Grow at 37 until an OD600 of approx. 0.1 (this is mid log

phase) then stick culture on ice to stop growth. At a 1:50 dilution of

the frozen this usually takes between 1.5 and 2.5 hours. An OD600 of

0.1=2x10e8 cfu/ml. Perform serial dilutions in sterile PBS to inject

5000 cfu iv for B6 mice (for balbs the typical dose is 2000). Peak CD8

response for primary is day 7 I think. I often got about 8-13% of CD8s

in the spleen responding by tetramer or IFNg.

I do everything is in a BSL2 TC hood. Use disposable plastic 1 ml

cuvettes to OD bugs and I parafilm around the top of them. Bleach all

tips and pipets, etc in 10% bleach before disposing as biohazard. When

I do FACS staining of cells from infected mice, I flick plates into a 2

L beaker with bleach in the bottom and always use a plate with a lid

(until fixed of course). I know this is overkill compared to Bevan Lab

procedures, but there's always someone pregnant around here\!

**Growing a stock**

Growing a Listeria stock

This is a protocol that I got from Maurice, Nicholas J (nmaurice@fredhutch.org) for the Lm-OVA variants (N4, Q4, T4)

Our former technician, who was a bit eccentric, wrote this (and it isn’t formatted for the N4/Q4/T4, which are chromosomally integrated) I’ll note where our practices differ in red:

Procedure

1.  Inject 3000-4000 CFU into hosts (I just start with the vial in \~3-4mL Brain-heart infusion (BHI) media, with no antibiotic and move to step 9)

2.  Sacrifice mice between 48 and 60 hrs post-infection

3.  Harvest and tease apart spleen in 5ml PBS+0.1% NP40

4.  Plate 200ml of spleen solution onto BHI+erythromycin plate \[spread out sequentially to get single colonies\]

5.  Incubate for 48 hours

> There should be little to no growth after 24 hours – the presence of large colonies at this time point suggests contamination.  
> Do not grow past 48 hours – overgrowth may occur

�. Harvest a single colony into 200ml of BHI+ erythromycin (in mc tube) Take several medium-sized colonies (growth will be heterogeneous)

7\. Grow for 4 hr with shaking 37C (set speed higher to ensure agitation in mc tubes) �. Inoculate 4ml of BHI (no antibiotic at this step) with LM-OVA from previous step

9.  Expand with shaking until broth is mildly cloudy

10. Add 4ml of bacteria to 26ml of BHI broth in a 50ml Falcon tube (or 200ml flask) (depending on growth kinetics, you could set this up as an overnight)

11. Expand with shaking for several hours until broth is cloudy, add erythromycin for the last 45-60min of culturing (once the OD is at about 0.6) (I don’t bother with erythromycin since these are stable/do everything sterile)

12. Read OD: should be at 0.6 – 0.8 before moving on to step 12 (OD can be pushed a little higher; if overgrowth occurs, just add BHI and allow to re-enter log-phase of growth)

13. Add 15ml of the 40% glyercol stock (40% glycerol stock is 20mL glycerol in 30mL BHI; I sterile filter mine and use fresh) to the 30ml of cloudy bacteria culture

14. mix gently on ice and aliquot 1ml in screw top cryotubes (keep on wet ice while aliquoting) (I like my LM to be on wet ice to facilitate glycerol uptake for a bit before moving them into the -80°C freezer).

15. Freeze -80C
